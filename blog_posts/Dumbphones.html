Thoughts on Dumbphones

Why I'm writing this

×

I went to a reading by author August Lamm for her new book:"You Don't Need a Smartphone: A Practical Guide to Downgrading Your Phone and Upgrading Your Life". I, like I think most people who get introduced to the idea of a dumbphone, am easily distracted. She is someone who was like me and had found a solution to it. She got rid of the thing that distracted her the most: her phone.


To help illustrate the point, there were two banners that had word clouds describing two scenarios: a life with and without a smartphone. Given then title of her book, you can guess which one was more appealing. Her heavy bias aside, she made a good case for why a smartphoneless life is great.


We currently use a 'Brick' device that blocks certain apps that we choose. I've created a schedule that blocks social media unless I tap my phone against the Brick. The brick stays on our fridge so I'm incapable of using it when I'm in the office. It's definitely helpful, and feels in the spirit of going the non-smartphone direction.


At the event there was a raffle for a dumbphone, and my wife actually won one! She decided to try it out since she also found the idea interesting and it would be a waste of a phone if she didn't at least give it a try. She has to order a physical SIM, but other than that she should be good to just start using it as she's a pastry chef and her job isn't super tech heavy.


Out with the New, In with the Old?


Like Aziz Ansari and Joe Pesci, I had to learn everything about dumbphones, so I found the dumbphone subreddit and looked at all the dumbphones on this dumbphone finder site. I also went to YouTube as I do when I'm trying to learn about a subject. I found that the sentiment of creators like SnazzyLabs and MKBHD were essentially saying 'You're buying more to do less'. MKBHD has explicitly mentioned that you simply need more discipline, and while I would love to be more disciplined, I'm currently not disciplined. I would want a solution that helps the current me and not the idealized version of myself. I do agree that I'm trying to buy my way out of a problem, but the problem is the hyper consumerism promoted by the very thing I was trying to escape. So I was back to square one.


Final Verdict


I ended up finding a video by Becca Farsace where she goes through what she likes and dislikes about dumbphones and she presented a compromise with OLauncher, which is an android launcher and changes the stock Pixel UI that I have on my phone. It replaces the app icons with a list of app names on the home screen, where I put all the "important" stuff.


I'm testing some of this stuff out and might change, but so far it's okay. I can still access the rest of the apps the way I normally would by swiping up and getting a long list of them. I can type and it automatically launches once it narrows down the app I need, which is nice.


I've been using the launcher for a couple weeks now. While I haven't mustered the courage to delete my social media apps it's definitely caused me to pause when I unlock my phone out of boredom (or to push back the knowledge that my time is finite and worry that I'm making the most of it). I kind of miss widgets for some weird reason, but I'm not itching to go back to the stock launcher.


I'll delete my apps at some point, but this is a good tempering for a smartphone-less life.
